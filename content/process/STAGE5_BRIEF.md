# Stage 5 (C1) brief for batch leads

Read this with BATCH_LEAD.md, WRITER.md, all of STYLE.md and FRAMEWORK.md §1, §2, §6 (stage 5 rows), §7, §9–12, §14. Read the last 15 units of stage 4 (content/stage4/b09.json, b10.json) before you place any scene.

## C1 shape
- Dialogues 7–10 lines, lines up to about 16 words, at least half of B's lines 2–8 words (STYLE §7.1–2). At most 30% new, unglossed dialogue words.
- About 4 items per normal lesson are receptive (STYLE §13): list their indexes in "receptive". Heritage words, nabaṭi lines, proverbs, formal-register set phrases and other voices are receptive. Receptive items appear only in A's lines.
- 9 items per normal lesson (8 is the floor, 7 is accepted after review deletions); checkpoints and exits 6.
- culture field in about 1 lesson in 2; every 5th unit (s5u005, s5u010 … s5u090) carries a weekly optional "task" (FRAMEWORK §11): longer than B2 (10-minute conversations, 2-minute monologues), a T1 conversation task at least every third task, I4 in at most 1 task in 5, and from s5u038 the tasks switch register on purpose. ≤400 characters, no URLs.
- Register labels: (formal), (respectful), (friends only), (ironic) go in the English column.

## Grammar at C1 (course-lead ruling, replacing the FRAMEWORK §6 note)
No full Emirati reference grammar is in hand: only its contents pages (the chapter list is the basis of the FRAMEWORK §6 stage 5 rows) and its phonology chapters. So:
- teach each C1 grammar point through **chunks**, not rules. The grammar label names the function ("hedging", "strong denial", "as for … , …"), and the tip describes how the chunk is used in the dialogue, never a general rule the course can't source;
- every productive chunk needs evidence: attest.py counts (both words and, where possible, the bigram), or the 1977 Gulf grammar or another source in /home/claude/refs. Cite it in your writer log, never in content;
- a chunk with thin evidence (bigram 0/0, or one source only) is either replaced by an attested chunk for the same function, or made receptive and put in your writer log's native queue. Never make the learner produce an unattested form;
- Kuwait-leaning forms (chūd, gid) are receptive and labelled, and only Fahad or Nūra say them.

## Lessons learned in stages 2–4 (each has caused errors)
- **Re-teaching is the most common error.** An item is a re-teach if it is a taught item with only the person, pronoun, article, negation, tense marker, or one noun / adjective / participle changed, or the same words in another order, or with or without il-. Grep every item (and its root, for single words) across content/stage1–5 before you keep it; finish with none of the linter's near-duplicate warnings. Adding a known word to a taught item, or swapping the verb in a taught frame, is allowed. Different set formulas are not re-teaches.
- **When a re-teach is found, delete the item and keep the phrase in the dialogue** (recycling is good). Don't replace it with a new multi-word chunk: new chunks are what keep producing fresh re-teaches. To top up a short unit, add a single content word whose root has never been an item in stages 1–5.
- **Recycling (FRAMEWORK §10.2):** every unit reuses at least 3 items from its previous 10 units and at least 2 from 30 or more units back, in its dialogue. Checkpoints reuse their block's items.
- The unit's grammar point appears at least twice in its dialogue.
- Every social formula has its reply from the other speaker in the same dialogue (the linter warns).
- The future needs b-; English glosses match the Arabic exactly (person, tense, number); a tip never names a form the dialogue doesn't use.
- Never teach a bare homograph (STYLE §2 list; the linter warns).
- Transliteration: the i drops before a vowel-initial ending (tkhallṣah, yistakhdmūnah, ṭarrshah); hyphenate t-h, s-h, k-h, d-h, g-h, c-h, z-h clusters (gilt-ha, ḥagg-hum); Form I/V/X imperfects keep their vowels (yighsilūn, biyitwassakh, biyisʼalūn). Check new words against STYLE §2–3.
- Emirati characters (Sēf, Maryam, Bū Rāshid, Rāshid, Umm Sēf) never use Kuwaiti or Saudi forms; Fahad and Nūra do, labelled.
- Story continuity: B has been in Dubai about three years, studies engineering and interns at the office with Maryam, has his own car, lives in Mirdif with a friend; Sēf has had his scholarship interview for a master's abroad (s4u108–s4u110); Rāshid (17) is Bū Rāshid's son. Check what stage 4 says before you place a scene, and don't recycle a stage 3 or 4 plot (lost car, late plane, desert-camp joke).
- No unsourced facts: every culture claim, custom, rule, date or figure is cited in your writer log from an official or major-news source, or left out. Heritage words (pearling, the dhow, falconry, the ʿizba) need a source for meaning; nabaṭi lines are quoted only if sourced, in transliteration if the Arabic script isn't.
- Mixed company follows STYLE §7 rule 11. The fuṣḥā column follows STYLE §8 (identical words are repeated).
- Spelling of shared words follows the earlier stages (English = إنقليزي, two = ثنين, ʿindana, tisʿ before a noun).
- Source names (books, courses, institutions) never appear in any content field; they go in your writer log only.

Writers have no Agent tool: do the write + self-check loop + writer log and stop; the course lead runs the reviews. Don't commit.
